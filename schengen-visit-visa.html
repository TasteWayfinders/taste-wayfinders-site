Schengen Visit Visa

One visa, 26 countries — what you need to know before applying for a short-stay Schengen visa for tourism or business.

90 daysWithin any 180-day period
26 countriesCovered by one visa
Apply atMain destination's embassy

A short-stay Schengen (type C) visa lets you travel across all 26 Schengen countries for tourism, family visits, or business — for up to 90 days in any 180-day period, on a single visa.

Key facts

Stay limit

Up to 90 days within any 180-day period, across the whole Schengen area — not per country.

Where to apply

At the embassy or consulate of your main destination country — or the country you'll enter first, if your trip is evenly split.

Documents you'll typically need

  • Valid passport (valid at least 3 months beyond your intended departure from Schengen)
  • Completed application form and recent photographs
  • Travel medical insurance (minimum €30,000 coverage)
  • Round-trip flight itinerary and accommodation proof
  • Bank statements and proof of ties to Nigeria

Document legalisation — Nigeria to Schengen

For most tourist applications, legalisation is less central than for study or civil matters — but if your file includes documents like invitation letters, marriage certificates, or business registration papers, the same rule applies:

CountryLegalisation route for Nigerian documentsNotes
FranceFull consular legalisationNigeria isn't a Hague Apostille member, so documents need Nigerian MFA authentication + French Embassy legalisation.
NetherlandsFull consular legalisationSame two-step chain: Nigerian MFA, then the Dutch embassy or consulate.
LithuaniaFull consular legalisationApostilles aren't accepted from non-Hague countries like Nigeria — full legalisation required.
PortugalFull consular legalisationAcademic and civil documents typically need MFA authentication plus Portuguese embassy legalisation.
SpainFull consular legalisationSpanish consular offices require the full legalisation chain for documents from non-Hague countries.
SwitzerlandFull consular legalisationSwitzerland is a Hague member, but since Nigeria isn't, documents still need full legalisation before use there.
BelgiumFull consular legalisationSame chain applies: Nigerian MFA authentication, then Belgian embassy legalisation.
ItalyFull consular legalisationItalian consulates require full legalisation for Nigerian-issued academic and civil documents.

All eight countries above are Hague Apostille Convention members — but because Nigeria itself is not a member, an apostille alone won't be accepted for Nigerian-issued documents in any of them. Instead, documents typically go through: (1) authentication at the Nigerian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, then (2) legalisation at the destination country's embassy or consulate in Nigeria.
