UK Standard Visitor visa: the basics

The Standard Visitor visa is for tourism, visiting family and friends, short business trips and some short courses. It is not for working or studying long term.

  • Length: usually up to 6 months per visit. Longer-validity visas (2, 5 or 10 years) exist, but you still stay a maximum of 6 months at a time.
  • Apply: online, then attend a biometrics appointment.
  • What the officer looks for: a clear reason for your trip, enough funds, and strong ties that show you will return home (job, business, family, property).
  • Documents: passport, bank statements, proof of income or business, travel plan, accommodation details. See our document checklists.

Canada study permit: what to prepare

Most international students need a letter of acceptance from a Designated Learning Institution (DLI) before applying for a study permit.

  • Acceptance letter: from a DLI. Some provinces also require an attestation letter.
  • Proof of funds: tuition plus living costs. The required amount is updated by IRCC, so check the current figure.
  • Study plan or letter of explanation: why this course, why Canada, and how it fits your future plans.
  • Other documents: passport, photos, academic records, language test results where required, medical exam where required.

Schengen visa: key points

The Schengen visa lets you travel across the Schengen countries for short stays, up to 90 days in any 180-day period.

  • Where to apply: the embassy or consulate of the country where you will spend the most time, or the first country you enter if the time is equal.
  • Insurance: travel medical insurance with the required cover is mandatory.
  • Itinerary: flight and accommodation reservations that match your plan.
  • Timing: appointments can be scarce, so apply as early as the embassy allows.

Common reasons for visa refusal

  • Weak ties to your home country, so the officer doubts you will return.
  • Unclear funds: large sudden deposits or statements that do not match your stated income.
  • Inconsistent information between forms, documents and interview answers.
  • Poor travel purpose: a vague or unrealistic itinerary.
  • Incomplete or unverifiable documents.

A refusal is not always the end. Understand the reason before you reapply, and do not resubmit the same application unchanged. Not sure where you stand? Try the free Visa Readiness Check.

Grenada citizenship: quick answers

  • Can I keep my current nationality? Yes, Grenada allows dual citizenship.
  • Do I have to live there? No residency requirement to qualify.
  • Can my family be included? Spouses and dependent children, and in some cases dependent parents.
  • Is approval guaranteed? No. The government runs due diligence on all adult applicants.
