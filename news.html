🇺🇸 USA — Two separate policies affect Nigerian applicants; know which is which

Still in effect: Presidential Proclamation 10998 (effective January 1, 2026) partially suspends entry for Nigerian nationals applying for B-1/B-2 visitor visas, F/M/J student and exchange visas, and immigrant visas. This is a separate, ongoing restriction — it has not been lifted.

Lifted: A different, narrower pause — on immigrant visa issuance for nationals of countries flagged for "public benefits reliance" screening, which Nigeria was also subject to from January 21, 2026 — was confirmed lifted by the State Department on August 21, 2026 following a court ruling in CLINIC et al. v. Rubio. No previously issued visas were revoked, and this specific pause never touched tourist (nonimmigrant) visas.

Also current: Nigeria is on the U.S. list of countries subject to visa bonds (up to $5,000–$15,000 for B-1/B-2 applicants) effective January 21, 2026, under a Temporary Final Rule — this is an active program, not a pilot.

🇬🇧 UK — eVisas now standard, plus current processing times

Since 25 February 2026, most visa nationals — including Nigerian applicants, per a statement from the UK High Commission in Abuja — who apply for a UK visit visa receive an eVisa only; no physical vignette sticker is issued. The application requirements and process itself are unchanged.

Processing times: for complete applications submitted outside the UK, UK Visas and Immigration's published standard is 90% decided within 3 weeks, 98% within 6 weeks, and 100% within 12 weeks for non-settlement visas (tourist, work, study). Settlement visas: 98.5% within 12 weeks, 100% within 24 weeks. Paid priority service typically returns a decision within 5 working days, and super-priority within 1 working day, where available.

🇨🇦 Canada — IRCC processing times for Nigerian applicants

In IRCC's most recent weekly figures (week of September 22, 2026): work permit processing held at roughly 10 weeks and study permit at roughly 10 weeks, both unchanged from the prior week. Visitor visa processing rose to about 93 days, up from 87 days. Super visa processing eased slightly to about 44 days, down from 46 days. IRCC typically refreshes its tool weekly, so treat these as a snapshot and always check the live tool for your specific application type and visa office before finalising travel plans.

Study permits in detail: for applications submitted from Nigeria, IRCC's estimate rose from 9 to 10 weeks in its 16 September 2026 update; applications made from inside Canada rose from 7 to 8 weeks. IRCC's formal service standards remain 60 days for applications made outside Canada and 120 days for those made inside Canada. These are estimates, not guarantees — incomplete files, extra document requests, and security checks can add time, so apply as early as your Letter of Acceptance allows.

🇪🇺 Schengen Area — Current visa requirement, plus EES and ETIAS status

Nigerian passport holders still require a Category C (short-stay) Schengen visa to enter any of the 29 Schengen countries — Nigeria is not on the EU's visa-exempt list, so the ETIAS travel authorisation (still delayed, with no confirmed launch date) does not apply to Nigerian travellers regardless. The EU's Entry/Exit System (EES), which digitally logs entries, exits and biometrics at the external border, has been live since 10 April 2026 and applies to short-stay travellers including Nigerians, in addition to the visa requirement. The standard visa fee is €90 for adults, and applications are handled through VFS Global centres in Lagos and Abuja.
